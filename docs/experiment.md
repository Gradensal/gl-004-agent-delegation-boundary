# Approval Burden Experiment

## Research Question

How does the placement of a delegation boundary affect the number of human
approval requests generated by a persistent AI agent?

## Hypothesis

A risk-based delegation policy can substantially reduce routine human
interruptions compared with an ask-everything policy while preserving explicit
approval and block boundaries for configured consequential actions.

This experiment does not test whether either policy is safer in real-world
deployment.

## Workload

A deterministic synthetic workday containing 100 proposed agent actions.

The workload includes:

- 75 low-risk internal actions
- 20 consequential actions
- 1 previously unknown action
- 4 explicitly prohibited actions

Both policies receive the exact same action proposals.

## Policy A — Ask Everything

Every action not explicitly blocked requires human approval.

This represents a conservative approval-heavy baseline.

## Policy B — Risk-Based Delegation

The deterministic delegation engine classifies actions as:

- ACT
- ASK
- BLOCK

using explicit policy conditions and action context.

## Results

### Ask-Everything

| Decision | Count |
|---|---:|
| ACT | 0 |
| ASK | 96 |
| BLOCK | 4 |

Approval burden: **96%**

### Risk-Based Delegation

| Decision | Count |
|---|---:|
| ACT | 75 |
| ASK | 21 |
| BLOCK | 4 |

Approval burden: **21%**

## Observed Difference

Risk-based delegation produced:

- **75 fewer human approval requests**
- a reduction from **96% to 21% approval burden**
- a **75 percentage-point difference**

## Audit Evidence

Each of the 100 actions was evaluated under both policies.

The experiment therefore generated:

**200 policy decisions**

The runtime JSONL trace is excluded from the public Git repository.

Summarized reproducible results are preserved in:

- `evidence/approval-burden-results.json`
- `evidence/approval-burden-results.md`

## Interpretation

The experiment demonstrates that delegation architecture can materially
change human interruption volume even when the underlying workload remains
identical.

It does not establish that lower approval volume is inherently safer,
better, or appropriate for production systems.

## Limitations

This experiment uses:

- synthetic actions
- hand-designed policy rules
- a simplified risk model
- no live applications
- no real human approval behavior
- no measurement of user attention
- no measurement of false approvals
- no adversarial agent behavior
- no production identity or authorization infrastructure

The results therefore describe the behavior of this prototype only.
